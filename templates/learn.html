How to spot phishing yourself

The checker is only a helper. These habits protect you everywhere, including places no tool can reach.

Six habits that stop most attacks

  1. Read the real domain. It is the last two words before the first single slash. In login.paypal.com.example.top the owner is example.top.
  2. Distrust urgency. "Act now", "account suspended" and "last warning" are designed to stop you thinking.
  3. Never share OTPs, PINs or passwords. A real bank or company will not ask for them by message or call.
  4. Go to the source yourself. Open the official app or type the website address instead of tapping the link.
  5. Check the sender, not just the name. A display name is easy to fake; the address behind it is harder.
  6. Be careful with app downloads. Install apps only from official stores, never from a link in a message.

What this tool cannot do

  • It does not open the link, so it cannot see what the page actually looks like.
  • It does not know about brand-new attacks. A clean score does not mean safe.
  • A rule can misfire. A genuine site with a long address or login-style words may gain a few points.

If you have already entered details on a suspicious page: change that password, contact your bank if money or cards are involved, and report it at cybercrime.gov.in or on 1930 in India.
